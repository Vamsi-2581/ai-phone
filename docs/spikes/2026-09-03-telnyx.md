# Spike 0a — Telnyx: a bought number, a registered daemon, a real call (2026-09-03)

**Question.** Does a Telnyx number bought with the user's own key reach a daemon registered from behind a home router,
and does a phone call to it hold a conversation? **Answer: yes**, after three account-level hurdles on the Telnyx side
and three defects on ours, all fixed on the branch the same day.

## Setup

- Number: a US local number (+1 660), bought in the portal on a fresh account. UK numbers need Level 2 verification.
- Connection: one credential connection (`ai-sip`), SIP user + password, Outbound Voice Profile `default`.
- Daemon: `ai-phone run --config ~/.ai-phone/spike-telnyx.yaml --tier t4` on an M5 Pro 24 GB, behind a Virgin Media
  hub, no port forwarding, no STUN, no `public_addr`. Line `main`: `kind: trunk`, `registrar: sip:sip.telnyx.com;transport=tcp`,
  `transport: tcp`, `accept_invites_from: registered_trunk` with Telnyx's twelve signalling /32s as `trusted_cidrs`.
- Password in `~/.ai-phone/secrets.env` as `AI_PHONE_LINE_MAIN_PASSWORD` (0600). Nothing else on the machine knows it.

## What Telnyx does (verified against the wire, not the docs)

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| REGISTER over UDP and TCP both get 401 → 200 in ~300 ms; the 200 carries `Contact …;expires=289`, so the 300 s we ask for becomes ~290 s and PJSIP refreshes at ~4.7 min | daemon log, both transports |
| Telnyx rewrites our Contact to the router's public address (`received`/`rport`) and PJSIP adopts it (`sdpNatRewriteUse`), so the SDP we answer with carries the public IP and RTP flows without STUN | 200 OK to the INVITE: `c=IN IP4 86.x.x.x`, 1.0K RTP packets each way, 0 lost, RTT 38 ms |
| Over TCP Telnyx sends the INVITE **down the registration's own connection**, so a NAT that drops unsolicited inbound packets is not in the way. The portal shows the registration (Authentication and routing → *Check registration status*: IP, transport, last registration) | INVITE arrived `from TCP 64.16.250.10:5060` on the socket we had opened |
| `sip.telnyx.com` resolves to several proxies; UDP registered at 192.76.120.10, TCP at 64.16.250.10, and the INVITE for a TCP registration came from the registrar we were connected to | log |
| The in-dialog Contact Telnyx gives us is a private address (`sip:mod_sofia@10.31.35.4:6000`); our BYE still went to the registrar over a new TCP connection and got 200 | log |
| Inbound calls to a **Pretrial** account are refused before routing; the caller hears silence then "user busy" | self-call from the trunk to its own number: `404 Not found D19` = "receiving account billing blocked"; portal notification "Account Disabled: Insufficient Balance" |
| **Trial** (after use-case verification): inbound only *from* the one verified number, outbound only *to* it, 10 min per call, 10 calls/hour | self-call: `403 Can not make calls to non-verified numbers at this account level D60`; docs |
| **Paid** (verified phone, service address, 2FA, one card payment): anyone can call the number; outbound still capped at 100/day, 10/hour, 5 concurrent | the conversation below |
| The portal's Web Dialer registers as a second device on the same credentials and takes inbound calls unless *Enable simultaneous ringing* is on (Inbound tab) | first inbound test rang the browser, not the daemon |
| SIP Call Flow Tool and Detail Record Search are paid-plan features; on trial the only debugging is our own log | portal |

`lines add telnyx --key-env TELNYX_API_KEY --country US --area 660` against the real API (22:07): the number search, the
journal, `resume` and `remove` all behaved; the account did not. Two account-level caps stopped the flow before any purchase:
`POST /outbound_voice_profiles` → `403 You may only have 1 Outbound Voice Profile(s) at your account level`, then
`POST /credential_connections` → `403 You may only have 1 Credential Connection(s) at your account level` (the account already
held `ai-sip` and a portal-made `Forward Only` connection). The provider now shares the account's existing profile when it
may not create one and never deletes a shared profile on `remove` (tested); the connection cap has no workaround short of the
account's next level, so the end-to-end provisioning proof (buy, register, remove, nothing left) moves to the Twilio spike
or to a Telnyx account whose connection slot the CLI may own. `lines remove gate` after the failure left the account exactly
as it was: one profile, two connections, one number.

## The call

A 68-second conversation from an iPhone on a UK mobile network to the US number, 13 turns, with barge-in.

| Turn | Caller said | Reply | first_audible |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | "Hey, can you hear me? Testing one, two, three." | "Yes, I can hear you clearly." | 1735 ms |
| 1 | "Nice, how you doing?" | "I'm doing great, thanks for asking!" | 916 ms |
| 2 | "Okay, so what can you help me with?" | "I can help you with a wide range of tasks…" | 996 ms |
| 6 | "I'm gonna test your interruption mechanism." | "I'm ready to test that mechanism whenever you like." | 1197 ms |
| 7 | "Okay, so give me the alphabet." | "Here is the alphabet: A B C D…" | 1476 ms |
| 8 | "Okay, whoa, whoa, whoa, slow down." (barge-in) | "I'm slowing down and ready to listen. [interrupted]" | 956 ms |
| 9–11 | "say the alphabet slower" / "Okay, wait." / "But slower." (three barge-ins) | each cut off within the barge-in budget | 795–957 ms |

Stage times on the steady turns: endpoint 280 ms, STT 70–210 ms, LLM first clause 290–560 ms, TTS first chunk 100–190 ms.
The first turn pays ~0.6 s more: the host was 12 GB into swap and the model weights come back out of the memory compressor
(see below). Latency on the PSTN leg is measured daemon-side exactly as on the LAN; the phone network adds its own ~150 ms
each way on top, which the caller feels and the record does not show.

## What broke, and what changed on the branch

1. **Incoming requests could land on the wrong line.** PJSIP picks the account for an incoming request by score
   (transport type 8, domain 4, To user 2, request-URI user 1). With the TCP-registered trunk and a UDP `sip` line side by
   side, a LAN INVITE for `sip:ai@127.0.0.1` was handed to the trunk, whose `registered_trunk` policy answered 403.
   Fix: `AiEndpoint.onSelectAccount` re-selects with `line_for_request` (request-URI/To user+host → sip line; source
   in the trunk's registrar/trusted set or the trunk's SIP user → trunk). Unit-tested; the override is logged.
2. **"It doesn't hear me."** On a 24 GB Mac running Docker Desktop's VM, a second VM, Chrome, two Claude Code sessions and
   Bitdefender (15–17 GB in swap), macOS paged the idle model workers out to under 50 MB resident. The first transcript
   after a quiet spell arrived 9–10 s after the caller stopped talking; the caller had hung up. Voice detection was fine
   (the recorded inbound audio ran through Silero offline: every utterance detected). Changes:
   - every MLX worker opens a wired limit before loading (`models.wire_memory`; measured to be a hint the kernel does not
     always honour under pressure, kept because it costs nothing where it works);
   - workers cap MLX's buffer cache at 64 MB and clear it after warm-up and after every request: STT footprint 2.8 → 1.5 GB
     (the float32 copy of the checkpoint is gone), LLM 3.9 → 2.8 GB;
   - idle workers run one tiny inference every `models.keep_warm_sec` (30 s): the weights stay in the compressor rather
     than on disk, so a turn pays ~0.3–0.8 s of decompression instead of 10 s of page-ins; the cadence backs off when a
     tick takes over a second so a real request never waits behind a slow tick;
   - `conversation.reply_timeout_sec` (12 s): if STT, the model or TTS is silent that long after the caller stops, the
     brain-error path runs (apology, listen again; goodbye on the second in a row) instead of silence until hangup.
3. **Trial-level 403 was explained as a caller-ID problem** by `ai-phone call`. The 403 hint now names both causes:
   a caller ID the carrier does not accept, or a destination the account level does not allow (Telnyx D60 on trial).

Also seen and not a defect: PJSIP's own log lines are written by C stdio and reach `daemon.log` late and in blocks when
stdout is a file, so the daemon's Python lines look "glued" into them. Run the spike with `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1` or read
the two streams separately.

## Outbound

- `ai-phone call +44…` (the owner's mobile) from the US number: Telnyx answered `403 … not included in whitelisted countries USA,CAN D13`.
  The account's one outbound voice profile allows US and Canada only; adding the UK required the account's next level, whose
  identity check the owner declined. Outbound to non-US/CA destinations on this Telnyx account is therefore out of scope for
  the gate; profiles the CLI creates now whitelist the number's own country as well (commit 98c8c11).
- `ai-phone call sip:ai@127.0.0.1:5062` from the `local` line to a pjsua peer that auto-answers and speaks (22:51): dialled,
  answered, the outbound opening played, the peer's sentence was transcribed and answered (first_audible 1696 ms on the first
  turn, 955 ms on the second), `ended: answered`, exit 0. The outbound path — claim, dial, early media held until CONFIRMED,
  opening line, turns, teardown — works carrier-free; on the trunk it is the same code with Telnyx's SIP in between.

## Forced drop (23:55, both trunks registered, Wi‑Fi switched off for 30 s)

| Line | Transport | What the log shows | Registered again |
|---|---|---|---|
| tw (Twilio) | UDP | its refresh fell inside the outage: `503 No working DNS nameserver` at 23:55:28 → DEGRADED, retry at 23:55:37 (first retry ≈ 5 s + jitter) still down, retry ≈ 30 s later succeeded | 23:56:10, **29 s** after the network returned |
| main (Telnyx) | TCP | no REGISTER failed; the dead TCP connection was noticed by the keepalive, PJSIP reconnected and re-registered out of cadence (23:54:53 → 23:57:09 instead of ~4.7 min) | 23:57:09, **88 s** after the network returned |

So the UDP line meets the gate's "recovered within 60 s"; the TCP line takes longer because nothing fails until the
keepalive write on the dead socket does (`sip.tcp_keepalive_sec: 30`). Lowering it to 10–15 s should bring TCP under a
minute at the cost of one tiny packet per line every 10–15 s; to be measured, not assumed, at the gate.
